# Careers and jobs while studying

Building a career can feel daunting. The good news is that there's plenty you can do during your degree, and much of it also looks great to future employers. Casual uni jobs and volunteering show "soft" skills like communication, planning, time management and leadership.

See also: [Placement: step by step](placement.md) · [What each major leads to](degree.md#the-cairns-majors)

## Jobs and roles at JCU

### PASS Leader

PASS leaders are students who run one-hour study sessions for subjects they've already done, helping other students with the content. It's a **paid casual job**.

- **Who can apply:** students who got a **Distinction or higher** in the subject.
- **How:** PASS leaders are usually recommended by lecturers. If you meet the requirement and you're interested, **email the subject coordinator** to say so.

### Student Mentor

Student mentors are students in **2nd year or higher** who are a friendly point of contact for first-years. It's a **volunteer role**, but it's fun and rewarding.

- You help at O-Week (e.g. running campus tours) and check in regularly with the first-years you're assigned to.
- **How:** let the current student mentors (or the student mentor program) know you're interested before the next O-Week.

### Student Ambassador

Student ambassadors are **paid casuals** who visit schools and help at JCU events like open days to promote studying at JCU. As an engineering student, you'll often be asked to run STEM activities for high schoolers. Past ambassadors say it's an incredibly fun job.

- **When:** intakes usually happen at the **start and middle of the year**, so keep an eye out.
- **Bonus:** approved **engineering** student ambassador work can count towards your placement. You apply for that separately through the Head of Engineering. See [service and leadership hours](placement.md#service-and-leadership-hours).

### Other casual jobs

The library and other parts of the university also hire students. Find these, and outside jobs and placements, on the **[JCU Careers hub](https://www.jcu.edu.au/careers)**.

### CES

Being on the CES executive or helping run events is great leadership experience, and it can count towards up to 3 weeks of placement. See [About CES](about.md#how-to-get-involved).

## Building your network

It's never too early to start.

- **Join [Engineers Australia](https://www.engineersaustralia.org.au/)** as a student member and go to their events. They're great for meeting local engineers.
- **Build a LinkedIn profile** and connect with people you meet, and join the [CES LinkedIn group](https://www.linkedin.com/groups/14317025/).
- **Talk to your lecturers.** Many have industry connections and know who's hiring.
- **Keep your resume up to date** with projects, casual jobs, volunteering and CES roles.
- **Use the JCU pre-approved placement host list** on LearnJCU as a list of companies to approach (see the [placement guide](placement.md#step-2-find-a-placement)).

## What happens if I don't get an internship?

You still need to complete the 12-week placement requirement to graduate, but it doesn't have to be one big internship. It can be several shorter placements, part-time work, approved engineering work in an existing job, and up to 3 weeks of service and leadership. If you're getting close to final year without a placement, **talk to the Student Placements Team ([placements@jcu.edu.au](mailto:placements@jcu.edu.au)) early**.
