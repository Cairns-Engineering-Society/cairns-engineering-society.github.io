## Overview

- **How much:** **12 weeks** of placement, which is **60 working days** or **480 hours**. JCU calls this your Engineering Professional Practice (EPP) requirement.
- **Why:** it's required for the degree to be accredited by Engineers Australia, and it means you graduate with real experience behind you.
- **Split it up:** you don't have to do it all at once. You can do several placements with different employers, and full-time and part-time both count.
- **Paid or unpaid:** both are fine.
- **Service and leadership:** up to **3 of the 12 weeks** can come from things like running CES events. See [Service and leadership hours](#service-and-leadership-hours).
- **Deadline:** you can't graduate until it's done. If you finish all your subjects without completing placement, your degree won't be awarded until you do, which can delay graduation or a graduate job.

## The process in order

### Step 1: Read the official requirements

On [LearnJCU](https://www.jcu.edu.au/learn), go to the **Organisations** tab and open **Engineering: Work Integrated Learning Including Professional Practice**. It has the official handbook, the forms you'll need, and advice on finding a placement.

### Step 2: Find a placement

Placements are usually **self-sourced**: you contact companies yourself.

- **Start with JCU's pre-approved hosts.** The list and contact details are in the LearnJCU organisation above. These companies already have an agreement with JCU, which **cuts down the paperwork**. The list is also a great networking tool.
- **Ask companies that don't advertise.** Many don't post placement positions but will take a student if you ask.
- **Check InPlace** ([inplace.jcu.edu.au](https://inplace.jcu.edu.au/)) for advertised opportunities you can apply for.
- **Check your supervisor is a degree-qualified engineer.** JCU requires this, and it's a mandatory question when you register the placement.

### Step 3: Register the placement in InPlace (before you start)

Log in to [InPlace](https://inplace.jcu.edu.au/) with your JCU login and submit a **Self Placement** from the To Do list on your home page. You'll need:

- the company's name, ABN, address, phone and website
- your supervisor's name, job title, email and phone, plus their engineering qualification (e.g. B.Eng, CPEng, RPEQ)
- start and end dates, number of days, hours per week, and whether it's paid or unpaid
- your role title and a description of the tasks you'll do (this is what JCU uses to decide whether the placement is suitable)
- a position or project description, and your CV (plus a cover letter if you have one)

**Tip:** the LearnJCU organisation has a self-sourced placement form with the same fields. Fill it in with the company first, then copy it into InPlace.

### Step 4: Wait for approval and paperwork

- JCU reviews the placement and tells you whether it's approved through InPlace.
- If the company doesn't already have an agreement with JCU, a **Student Placement Agreement** and a **health and safety form** need to be signed by the company and JCU. This usually takes **5–10 working days**.
- **Don't start until you get the placement confirmation email.** Work done before approval may not count.

### Step 5: Do the placement and keep your logbook

- Keep a **weekly logbook** (template in the handbook). Update it every 1–2 days while you still remember what you did.
- Your **supervisor signs it weekly**, or after every 30–50 hours of work.
- Submit the logbook **at the halfway point and again at the end** of your placement.

### Step 6: Write your report

Write a 1,500-word report on your placement. Your **supervisor must read and approve it** before you submit it. See [Your placement report](#your-placement-report).

### Step 7: Submit

Email the following to the **Student Placements Team** at [placements@jcu.edu.au](mailto:placements@jcu.edu.au) as soon as possible after your placement:

- your report
- the **Engineering Professional Practice Certificate**, signed by your supervisor (they also write at least 100 words about how you went)
- your service and leadership hours log, if you're claiming any

Once it's approved, your placement requirement is done. 🎉

## What counts towards your hours

- Engineering internships
- Vacation work with a mining, energy, construction, defence or technology company
- Working on engineering projects under the supervision of an engineer
- Approved engineering work as part of a job you already have
- **Service and leadership**, up to 3 weeks (see below)

**What doesn't count:** site visits, guest lectures and industry projects that are part of your normal subjects. They're valuable, but they're separate from the 12 weeks.

**Don't assume it'll be approved.** Every placement has to meet the requirements and be approved and documented. Check before you start.

## Service and leadership hours

Many students don't know about this. **Up to 3 weeks** of your 12 can come from service and leadership activities. The remaining **9 weeks or more** must be external industry placement.

**What counts:**

- **Office-bearing roles in student clubs and societies** where you show leadership, such as organising and running events. Being on the CES executive counts.
- **Volunteering with a student club or society** where you make a significant, documented contribution.
- **Being a student ambassador**, if you're approved to be one. This includes representing JCU or engineering at school visits, open days and community events, and supervising visiting school students. To apply, email [HeadEngineering@jcu.edu.au](mailto:HeadEngineering@jcu.edu.au) with your CV and a short statement on why you'd like to be an ambassador.

**How the hours convert:**

| Activity time | Counts as |
|---|---|
| 6 hours | 1 day |
| 5 days (30 hours) | 1 week |
| 90 hours | 3 weeks (the maximum) |

**What you can claim:** the whole activity, including planning, preparation, required travel, running it, packing up, reflection and writing it up. For example, a BBQ might be 1 hour of shopping and prep, 30 minutes of travel, 1.5 hours cooking and 30 minutes cleaning up, so 3.5 hours.

**Keep a log as you go.** Record the date, the hours, and a short description of each activity. Use our **[blank hours log template (Excel)](files/Service-and-Leadership-Hours-Log-Template.xlsx)**. It adds up your hours and converts them to days and weeks for you.

## Your placement report

The handbook says **one report covers your whole placement requirement**, including all your placements and any service and leadership hours. If you do several placements, check with the Student Placements Team whether they want a report after each one.

**Length:** about 1,500 words. You don't need detailed technical information, but you can refer to technical work and drawings to explain what you did.

**Sections:**

1. **Title page:** your name, major and year of study.
2. **Introduction:** what the company does, the area you worked in, and the economic, community and environmental factors that affect it.
3. **Placement summary:** your roles and projects, with dates and hours claimed. Include your evidence (certificates and your service and leadership log).
4. **Reflection on Engineers Australia Stage 1 Competencies:** how your placement developed specific competencies. **Do four**, including one from each of the three groups (Knowledge and Skill Base, Engineering Application Ability, and Professional and Personal Attributes). The handbook isn't consistent on whether three or four are needed, so four is the safe choice. A table works well.
5. **Conclusion**
6. **Appendices**, if needed

**Formatting:** Times New Roman or similar, 12 pt, justified, 1.5 line spacing, 25 mm margins. Clear, concise writing with professional diagrams.

**How it's marked:**

| Part | Weight |
|---|---|
| Industry supervisor report (the certificate) | 40% |
| Your report: placement summary | 30% |
| Your report: reflection on EA Stage 1 Competencies | 30% |

## A good placement timeline

Assuming a standard 4-year degree:

| Year | Aim |
|---|---|
| **Year 1** | Settle into uni and work out which major interests you. |
| **Year 2** | Start going to networking events and building connections. If you land an internship this year, that's awesome! |
| **Year 3** | Aim to have an internship by now. Most companies look for students in third year or above. |
| **Year 4** | Finish your placement and paperwork so it's done before you graduate. |

**Summer is your best chance.** In 3rd and 4th year, your first subjects of the year are block subjects that start in Block 2. In 2027 that's 30 March, compared with 15 February for trimester subjects. That leaves a longer break for placement over summer after 2nd and 3rd year.

**It's never too early to start building your network.** Struggling to find a placement as you get close to your final year? Talk to the Student Placements Team early. Don't wait.

## Tips from past students

- **Use the pre-approved host list as a networking list.** Those companies already work with JCU students.
- **Get on LinkedIn and go to Engineers Australia events.** A lot of placements come from people you've met.
- **Log your service and leadership hours as you go**, not months later. Use the [template](files/Service-and-Leadership-Hours-Log-Template.xlsx).
- **Fill in your logbook every day or two.** It's painful to reconstruct weeks of work at the end.
- **Don't start work before you get the confirmation email.** If you do, those hours might not count.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to do all 12 weeks in one go?**

No. You can combine several placements with different employers, and full-time and part-time work both count.

**Can my current job count?**

Possibly. Approved engineering work as part of an existing job can count if it meets the requirements, you're supervised by a degree-qualified engineer, and it's approved through InPlace.

**Does it have to be paid?**

No. Paid and unpaid placements both count. If you're unpaid, JCU's insurance covers you on an approved placement. Your own car isn't covered by JCU's insurance, so check with the company before using it for work.

**Can CES count towards my placement?**

Yes. Office-bearer roles and significant volunteering with CES can count towards up to 3 weeks. See [Service and leadership hours](#service-and-leadership-hours).

**What if I can't find a placement?**

Talk to the Student Placements Team as early as possible. They can give advice and point you to companies that take JCU students.

**Can I start before my placement is approved?**

No. Wait until you get the placement confirmation email.
