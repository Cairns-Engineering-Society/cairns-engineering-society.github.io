## Degree structure

| | Single degree | Double degree |
|---|---|---|
| **Degree** | BEng (Honours) | BEng (Honours) + Bachelor of Science **or** Bachelor of Information Technology |
| **Length (minimum)** | 4 years | 5 years |
| **Subjects** | 32 subjects (96 credit points) | 40 subjects (120 credit points) |
| **What you choose** | 1 major + 1 minor (or 4 electives) | 1 engineering major + 1 major from the other degree (no minor) |

Both include an **Honours thesis** in final year and **60 days of work placement** (see the [placement guide](placement.md)). The engineering degree is **accredited by Engineers Australia**.

**What each year looks like (single degree):**

| Year | What you study |
|---|---|
| 1st | The **same 8 subjects for everyone**. You choose your major (and minor) by the end of the year. |
| 2nd | Major subjects, plus minor or elective subjects |
| 3rd | Major subjects, plus minor or elective subjects |
| 4th | Major subjects, minor or elective subjects, and your Honours thesis |

In a double degree, years 2–5 mix engineering subjects with subjects from your second degree, with the thesis in 5th year.

## Majors, minors and electives

- **Major:** the engineering discipline you study, e.g. Electrical. It appears on your testamur (degree certificate).
- **Minor:** 4 subjects focused on another area, e.g. Software. Only available in the single degree. It doesn't appear on your testamur.
- **Electives:** instead of a minor, you can pick any 4 subjects at **level 2 or higher**. Level is the first digit of the subject code: EG2008 is level 2, MA3405 is level 3.

## The Cairns majors

### Electrical and Electronic Engineering

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** EG1012 Electric Circuits and MA1003 Mathematical Techniques.

- The theory can be very maths-heavy, with lots of circuit analysis.
- You'll build and fine-tune circuit designs. Projects during the degree have included a CNC milling machine, a sorting machine and an electronics design project.
- Compared with IoT, Electrical goes deeper into electromagnetic theory and the large-scale electrical infrastructure that powers everything.

**Careers around Cairns:** energy (Ergon), renewable energy (Barron Gorge hydro power station), defence (BAE Systems), aviation, telecommunications, buildings.

**What you might do:** design power systems, renewable energy systems, control systems and electrical infrastructure.

[Official handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHEEL)

### Electronic Systems and Internet of Things (IoT) Engineering

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** EG1012 Electric Circuits, MA1003 Mathematical Techniques and EG1002 Computing and Sensors.

- Combines electronic engineering with IT, software development, sensor technology, cloud data and AI.
- Key subjects include sensor technologies, statistical data mining for big data, and neural networks.
- Compared with Electrical, IoT focuses more on smaller-scale electronics, data science, and sensor design and control.

**Careers around Cairns:** environmental monitoring, agriculture, marine, energy, automation, research, defence.

**What you might do:** build sensor systems, program devices, analyse data, connect equipment and automate processes.

[Official handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHIOT)

### Mechanical Engineering (from 2027)

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** PH1005 Newtonian Physics, EG1011 Statics and Dynamics and EG1000 Engineering 1.

- Learn to analyse and design machines across many industries: fluid mechanics, the dynamics of machine elements, and material properties.
- You'll design machines, specify components, calculate loads and solve problems. Think of it as designing the car, rather than fixing it.

**⚠️ Townsville residentials:** Cairns students must attend the Townsville campus for three subjects in 3rd and 4th year: **ME3511** (Trimester 2), **ME4513** (Block 2) and **ME4522** (Trimester 2).

**Careers around Cairns:** marine (Austal, Norship), aviation, manufacturing, defence, construction, tourism, engineering consulting (e.g. AECOM, Arup).

**What you might do:** design machinery and components, investigate failures, inspect equipment and manage projects.

[Official handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHMEN)

### Maritime Engineering

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** PH1005 Newtonian Physics and EG1010 Process Engineering.

- Learn about the design, construction and maintenance of marine vessels and marine infrastructure, offshore energy, ocean research, and environmental management.
- **⚠️ Only the first 2 years are in Cairns.** For 3rd and 4th year you transfer to the **Australian Maritime College (University of Tasmania) in Launceston** to finish a Bachelor of Maritime Engineering (Honours), specialising in either:
  - **Naval Architecture:** designing everything from submarines and naval ships to yachts, ferries and cargo vessels.
  - **Ocean Engineering:** offshore, subsea and coastal structures, wave mechanics, underwater technology and offshore renewable energy.
- You'll also study two subjects online with the Australian Maritime College while in Cairns (JEE225 Hydrostatics and JEE246 Introduction to Maritime Science, Engineering and Technology).
- **Only available for Trimester 1 intake.** Discuss your study plan with the course coordinator in 1st year.
- **Changed your mind about moving?** If you complete the first two years and don't want to go to Tasmania, you can transfer into the **Mechanical** major. Talk to the course coordinator.

**Careers around Cairns:** reef tourism, commercial fishing, luxury yacht construction and other specialist marine industries.

**What you might do:** hydraulic modelling, structural analysis and stability calculations, vessel design, and supervising testing and construction.

[Official handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHMTE) · [UTAS Maritime Engineering](https://www.utas.edu.au/study/undergraduate/maritime-engineering)

## Majors only offered in Townsville

From 2nd year, these are only taught in Townsville, but there are plenty of jobs for them around Cairns.

### Civil Engineering

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** EG1011 Statics and Dynamics and EG1000 Engineering 1.

Design, build and maintain the infrastructure society runs on: structural, geotechnical, hydraulic, transport and environmental engineering. **Careers around Cairns:** structural, geotechnical and water resources engineering (e.g. Cairns Regional Council). [Handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHCVL)

### Chemical Engineering

**You might like it if you enjoyed:** EG1010 Process Engineering and PH1005 Newtonian Physics.

Use chemistry, maths and physics to design and run processes that produce things on a large scale. A chemist might develop a substance in a lab, and a chemical engineer works out how to produce it reliably at industrial scale. **Careers around Cairns:** water and environmental processing (e.g. Cairns Regional Council), waste treatment, and manufacturing (food, beverages, distilleries, pharmaceuticals). [Handbook page](https://handbook.jcu.edu.au/aos/2027/BEHCEM)

## Changing your major or campus

Changing your mind is normal. Plenty of students switch majors, and some switch more than once. Do what interests you, and talk to your lecturers to check you're making a sound decision.

1. **Get permission from the course coordinator first.** They approve changes to your major, campus or degree structure. [PLACEHOLDER: how students find the course coordinator's contact details]
2. **Then email the enrolments team** at [enrolments@jcu.edu.au](mailto:enrolments@jcu.edu.au). They make the change in the system.

## Study load and block subjects

- **Full-time load:** 8 subjects a year is the normal full-time load.
- **Block subjects** are compressed into about **6 weeks** instead of a full trimester. It's best to do **no more than two block subjects at a time**.
- **In 3rd and 4th year**, your first subjects of the year are block subjects starting in **Block 2**. In 2027 that's **30 March**, compared with **15 February** for trimester subjects. This gives you a longer summer break for [placement](placement.md#a-good-placement-timeline).
